Keeping children safe

Safeguarding & Child Protection

Last updated: 26 October 2026

The safety, dignity and wellbeing of every child at The Success Academy & School System comes first. Safeguarding and child protection mean protecting children from abuse, neglect, bullying, harassment, exploitation and harm, and acting quickly when there is a concern. This page explains our commitments and how to raise a concern.

1. Our commitments

  • Every child has the right to feel safe, respected and valued at school.
  • We do not allow physical punishment, humiliation, threats or any form of abuse of students.
  • We take bullying, including online bullying, seriously and act to stop it.
  • We treat every concern about a child’s safety seriously, whoever raises it.
  • We keep information about concerns confidential and share it only with the people who need to know in order to protect the child.
  • No one will be treated unfairly for raising a concern honestly and in good faith.

2. Staff responsibilities

  • All staff are expected to treat students with respect and to behave professionally at all times.
  • Any member of staff who has a concern about a child must report it promptly to school leadership.
  • Staff must not keep concerns about a child’s safety to themselves.

3. Online safety and photos

  • This website does not publish students’ private contact details.
  • Photos from school events appear in the gallery. If you do not want a photo of your child shown, contact us and we will remove it promptly.
  • We encourage parents to talk with their children about staying safe online and to tell a trusted adult if something worries them.

4. Concerned about a child?

If you are worried about a child at our school, whether you are a parent, student, member of staff or visitor, please contact the school leadership using the details below. You may speak with us in person, by phone, or by email. Please share what you know, including what happened, when and who was involved, as far as you are able to.

We will listen, take the concern seriously, and act on it promptly and sensitively. Where needed, we will involve parents or guardians and the appropriate authorities.

5. Emergencies

If a child is in immediate danger, call the police on 15 straight away. Please do not use the website forms for urgent safety matters.

6. Reviewing this page

We review this page from time to time and will update it when our practices change. The “Last updated” date above shows the latest version.
